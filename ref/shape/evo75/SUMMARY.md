# Evo75 silhouette and mount summary

## Shape to borrow

Treat the Evo75 as a deliberately layered case. The upper shell reads as a substantial, rounded rectangular box with a crisp, nearly level deck and a continuous perimeter seam. The lower body pulls inward beneath that shell, making a distinct wedge-like shadow line instead of a plain vertical slab. In side view, the shell remains visually thick and horizontal while the underside descends toward the rear; the transition is a smooth graduated contour, not a separate foot or an abrupt single bevel. Keycaps sit visibly above the shell and reinforce the clean, low front edge. The side profile in the Keypro gallery is the best static proportion reference; the alexotos review calls out the “graduated curve” at 05:35–05:42.

For chasm, the user’s stated design translation is each half as a “mini Evo75,” with Neo75 as the clean-line cue: retain this layered box-over-wedge side language and soft corner treatment, while scaling the plan silhouette to each half and using simpler uninterrupted lines.

## Numbers to anchor a model

- Footprint: 323.35 mm long × 143.42 mm wide.
- Fixed deck/typing inclination: 8°.
- Front height: 19.3 mm published; approximately 20 mm in the alexotos spoken measurement.
- Rear height: not located in published material reviewed here.
- Mass: listed 1997 g.

An 8° plane over the full 143.42 mm width would rise about 20.2 mm (143.42 × tan 8°). This is a geometric implication, not a measured rear-case height: the typing angle may refer to the plate/deck datum and the case underside has a curved/tapered construction. Do not use that calculation as a substitute for an actual rear-height dimension.

## Graduated curve estimate

The local video frames around 05:39 and 04:29 are useful for visual contour but are oblique camera shots rather than calibrated orthographic drawings. Using the published 143.42 mm front-to-back dimension only as a rough scale and the visible profile, the lower-case rise appears concentrated over approximately the rear third to half of the depth (roughly 50–75 mm), beginning around mid-depth and reaching its highest rear taper near the back edge. The contour is shallow and blended; a single constant radius is a poor fit. A practical CAD starting point is a tangent blend with an effective radius on the order of 100–180 mm, or a shallow 8–12° rear-up tangent over the last 50–75 mm. These are explicitly visual estimates, not published radii or angles; perspective and occlusion make tighter numerical claims unreliable. Trace the downloaded 1080p side frame or vendor side image in CAD before freezing the section.

## Butterfly leaf-spring mount

The board uses a butterfly leaf-spring mount, with metal leaf springs visible at the upper case and silicone spring/gasket pieces and cushions in the support stack. The PCB is 1.2 mm and has flex cuts; the plate option is PP or FR4. The reviewer describes the metal leaves as being on top and the base engaging from below (12:22–12:49), while vendors call out silicone gaskets/cushions and triple-layer shock absorption. This suggests a compliant plate/PCB assembly supported at multiple points by paired, shaped spring leaves and elastomer contact layers, combining vertical flex with lateral constraint. No reviewed source publishes the leaf count, exact alloy, thickness, plan shape dimensions, or support coordinates. Do not invent these as manufacturer specs; inspect frames 00-08-41, 00-12-23, and 00-12-27 for the closest visual guidance.
